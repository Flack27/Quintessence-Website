I'll go over the class's PvE and PvP strengths and weaknesses in a short summary. After that, feel free to explore the sub-sections below for advice on making your Templar shine in the early-to-mid game — by the time you reach end game you should have enough knowledge to fine-tune the build for yourself.

## PvE

**Pros**

- The one and only tank in the game.
  - Templar can take aggro, pull mobs, provide ATK buffs via passives, and provide shields via passives and skills.
  - Relatively safe and easy progression from level 1 to 45.
- Easier to get into party content due to the lack of tank players.

**Cons**

- You have to keep threat generation in mind — you can't just randomly press buttons. If a DPS pulls aggro, it usually points to a rotation issue, unless they simply have a lot more Gear Score than you.
- Solo farming or clearing certain daily/weekly bosses can be slower and more painful.
  - For solo farming in the open world, your best friend is **Poach** with the instant cooldown-reduction add-on — it refreshes on cooldown when the target dies to you. If someone else last-hits the mob, you won't get the refresh, so plan your pulls around that. Killing a mob nearby also refreshes the cooldown.
  - Daily/weekly dungeons require careful attention to mechanics so you don't lose even more of the DPS you're already short on.

## PvP

**Pros**

- Best 1v1 kit you can ask for.
  - CC (3x stuns, stagger, knockdown, 2x pull/grab), damage reduction, shield buffs, dashes (x2), CC resist, 3x uses of Shield Block (insane), high HP & defense, and add-ons providing small heals.
- Has two roles in large-scale PvP: **Frontline Tank** (survive and disrupt) and **Backline Peeler** (disrupt).
  - The roles mirror TnL. It's unclear how well a hybrid works, but if you've played TnL tanks, you'll recognize Blood Devotion here too — potentially the biggest strength for a Backline Peeler. Use it carelessly while your healer/DPS is under heavy pressure and you can accidentally kill them both, so stick to regular CC and shield buffs unless you're sure.

**Cons**

- Can be kited by ranged classes fairly easily.
  - You need to know other classes by heart to perform well against them — for example, when to use a CC break versus Shield of Protection to mitigate burst ("bomba") skills.
- Requires a team-first mindset — you can't play egoistically. Your entire job is leading the group (shotcalling or engaging fights) and peeling for your team.

### Reasoning

DPS players can easily reach 70% Smite and above 80% Perfect Chance — near impossible to counter both at once (this is worse on TW & KR than on Global, but still high enough that we can't fully build around it).

It's possible to build enough Critical Damage Tolerance just from the cape stat alone. The only real counter to the Smite meta is stacking enough Time stat — realistically we can only reach around 28.4% Smite Resistance.

## Pets

Each time you kill a monster that has a soul, you get it. Every pet starts at level 0, and each soul dropped raises its level from 0 up to level 5. Each level gives a bonus to the character — faster mount movement, more health, more evasion, and so on.

**Pet farming thresholds:**

- Unlock lvl 1 — kill 1
- Unlock lvl 2 — kill 15
- Unlock lvl 3 — kill 50
- Unlock lvl 4 — kill 100
- Unlock lvl 5 — kill 180

Genus insight also starts at level 0 and goes up to level 10 max. At lower levels you only get gray and green bonuses; blue bonuses appear later, and unique/heroic bonuses show up in the later stages.

The goal is to keep leveling your Genus insight until it hits level 10, then start rerolling the stats — don't lock in random blue/unique upgrades early.

**Target Genus layout:**

| Cogni | Fera | Natura | Varian | Special |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| Attack bonus | Attack bonus | Attack bonus | Attack bonus | Attack bonus |
